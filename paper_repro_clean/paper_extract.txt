Reliable Multipath and Multisource Content
Transmission and Caching for Information-Centric
Internet of Things
Xiaonan Wang
, Yajing Song
, and Hongbin Cheng
Abstract—Real-world content produced by Internet of Things
(IoT) requires real-time transmission to satisfy high-quality
surveillance. While information-centric networking (ICN) can
achieve rapid content access through in-network caching and
aggregation, it does not provide forwarding information on
backup providers that perform in-network caching. This lim-
itation leads to content unavailability and single-point failure.
Moreover, node resource depletion may cause path disruptions,
which disables aggregation and results in content transmission
failures. Taking these issues into account, we propose a multipath
and multisource content transmission and caching solution. The
solution leverages path weights to establish stable multipaths
toward optimal providers, sharing content among users. The
experimental results justify the feasibility and superiority of the
proposed solution.

I. INTRODUCTION
N
OWADAYS, the Internet of Things (IoT) has emerged
as a critical means for real-world data acquisition and
transmission, particularly for monitoring applications where
real-time content delivery is essential to ensure high-quality
surveillance [1], [2], [3]. IoT employs the address-based
communication model to deliver content, but this end-to-
end model cannot achieve rapid content transmission [4].
First, content unavailability and single-point failure frequently
arises because content is only retrieved from a speciﬁc
node with limited resources. Second, end-to-end transmission
is independently performed between a source node and a

request common content (e.g., environmental data in a speciﬁc
area), conventional end-to-end transmission proves inefﬁcient
as it requires nU independent delivery processes, leading to
considerable content delivery delays and energy consumption
through redundant transmissions.
Information-centric networking (ICN) [5] enhances content
delivery efﬁciency through two key mechanisms [6], [7],
[8]: 1) in-network caching that reduces content-user distances
by leveraging multiple sources, and 2) aggregation that facil-
itates data sharing among users. Therefore, we integrate ICN

A. Problem and Motivation
As ICN was not originally designed for IoT environments,
ICIoT needs to address several challenges to effectively
implement in-network caching and aggregation. First, in ICN,
forwarding information base (FIB) only collects forwarding
information on original producers [9], [10], [11], and does not
provide forwarding information on backup caching providers.
This leads to two drawbacks: 1) suboptimal utilization of in-
network caching that could otherwise minimize content-user
distances, and 2) content unavailability and single-point fail-
ures (SPF). Second, IoT nodes have limited energy, and energy
depletion induces path disruptions that disable aggregation and
cause content transmission failures. Third, ICN employs the
strategy of caching everything everywhere [12], [13]. That is,
content routers cache all received data. IoT nodes have limited
storage resources, and this caching strategy is inefﬁcient in IoT
environments due to three reasons: 1) Cached content becomes
unavailable after its short lifetime; 2) IoT nodes cannot provide
cached content after energy depletion; and 3) IoT nodes have
to frequently remove old content for new content.
To address these issues, we propose a Low-delay Multipath
and Multisource content transmission and caching solution
(LMM). To minimize content-user distances through in-
network caching and eliminate SPF, the proposal explores
multipaths toward multisources, including both original pro-
ducers and backup providers. To enhance path stability and
enable aggregation, the proposal evaluates node resources
to construct reliable multipaths toward optimal providers.
To enhance content availability and caching efﬁciency, the
proposal assesses both node resources and content features to
optimize in-network caching decisions.

II. RELATED WORK
In this section, the related work on address-centric IoT and
ICIoT is discussed.
A. Address-Centric IoT
Chanak and Banerjee [14] applied IoT to smart healthcare,
and present a healthcare content delivery solution. The solution
employs a priority-based scheduling strategy to control con-
gestion and enhance delivery ratios. In [15], an address-centric
content transmission method is proposed. The method ﬁrst dis-
covers network topology and uses multidimensional metrics to
evaluate node reliability and establish stable routing paths for
content delivery. Wang and Chen [16] presented an IoT data
transmission method. The method leverages learning automata
to probe routing paths, continuously disseminating data with-
out requests. Saxena et al. [17] introduced edge computing to
IoT, addressing resource limitations and minimizing end-to-
end transmission delays. The solution Leverages the resources
of edge devices to help transmit data, and the numerical results
demonstrate transmission efﬁciency. Jha et al. [18] presented
an end-to-end IoT dissemination method. The method adopts
clustering techniques to construct IoT, and uses energy levels
and neighbor metrics to select cluster heads, aiming to reduce
energy consumption and data access delays. Achour [19]
presented an anycast-based content transmission method. The
method employs anycast to deliver content between users and
optimal anycast members, aiming to reduce content response
time. In the method, each user fetches content independently
from an optimal anycast member. If the member is incapable
of providing content, the content transmission fails.
The above methods have demonstrated that the address-
centric mechanism can perform end-to-end content delivery
for IoT. However, in IoT applications where multiple users
request the same content, address-centric end-to-end delivery
proves inefﬁcient, leading to signiﬁcant energy consumption
and delays. Additionally, these methods suffer from content
unavailability, as data can only be retrieved from a speciﬁc
node.
B. Information-Centric IoT
The ICN standard [5] deﬁnes FIB, pending interest table
(PIT) and content store (CS) to perform content transmission.
Users send Interest with a name to fetch content. If nodes
receiving Interest have target content in CS, they send Data
back to users. If Interest hits their PIT, they wait for Data.
If Interest hits neither CS nor PIT, reverse paths are built
by creating PIT and Interest is forwarded toward producers
based on FIB. During the transmissions of Data, nodes
along reverse paths cache Data in their CS to serve future
requests [20], [21].
Araújo et al. [22] utilized ICN to mitigate request ﬂooding
and minimize transmission energy consumption. The method
implements multicriteria trafﬁc monitoring to detect ﬂooding
attacks and ﬁlter malicious requests. Saha et al. [23] deployed
ICN in IoT, aiming to improve transmission efﬁciency. The
proposed method integrates push-based and pull-based trans-
mission strategies while leveraging broadcast communication
for IoT data delivery, thereby enhancing delivery ratios. Wang
and Lu [9] leveraged ICN to access rapidly emergency data.
The method probes optimal paths by employing node charac-
teristics, facilitating efﬁcient data sharing among consumers.
Arsalan et al. [24] combined ICN with IoT and present
a content transmission solution to optimize content deliv-
ery efﬁciency. The solution exploits FIB to deliver content,
but it does not address the FIB establishment issue. Li et
al. [25] presented an underwater content transmission solution.
The solution integrates ICN with underwater networks to
minimize the distance between users and underwater data,
thereby reducing content delivery latency. The solution extends
ICN by incorporating a push-based delivery model with in-
network caching capabilities. Ud Din et al. [26] proposed
the concept of the neighbor FIB (NFIB) to transmit content.
Each node utilizes NFIB to forward Interest toward potential
content providers. Upon receiving Data, nodes store it, without
evaluating content characteristics.
These works have shown that the in-network caching and
aggregation can help enhance the efﬁciency of IoT-based
content delivery. However, these works have the following
limitations: 1) FIB does not reﬂect forwarding information
on backup caching providers, leading to content unavailability
and SPF; 2) Resource deﬁciency can cause path disruptions,
preventing aggregation and leading to transmission failures;
and 3) The ubiquitous caching strategy proves inefﬁcient when
resource-constrained nodes store transient content. Taking
these limitations into account, we propose a multipath and
multisource content transmission and caching for ICIoT, and
aim to reduce content transmission delays and failures. The
comparisons between the proposal and existing solutions are

providers SN2 and SN2′′. During Interest transmission, nodes

node SN1, as shown in Fig. 4(b). Since node EN0 has the
PIT entry for a1, aggregation is implemented. Thus, users U1
and U2 share content through aggregation, and node SN1′′

Since node SN2 is incapable of providing content c1, user U3
retrieves content c1 from optimal provider SN1′′, eliminating
SPF, and meanwhile EN0 updates its path table according to
path weights.

This superior performance stems from two key architectural
advantages. First, LMM employs a weighted multipath mech-
anism that: 1) utilizes node weights to acquire forwarding
information from multiple sources and 2) leverages path
weights to select optimal, stable paths, enabling aggregation
and avoiding transmission failures caused by both SPF and
resource exhaustion. In contrast, CIDF and EEDA are unaware
of forwarding information on caching providers. Second,
LMM exploits caching weights to optimize content caching,
reducing content-user distances. Moreover, LMM selects
optimal paths to enable aggregation, achieving rapid and
energy-consumption content sharing. While CIDF supports
aggregation, its performance is limited by two critical short-
comings: 1) resource deﬁciency frequently causes reverse-path
disruptions, leading to both aggregation failures and content
delivery failures and 2) content caching operates without
considering content characteristics. EEDA implements neither
aggregation nor in-network caching, requiring each user to
independently retrieve content.
The proposed approach implements in-network caching by
harnessing node resources and content features, distributing
backup providers across the IoT network. As a result, the
distance between users and optimal backup providers remains
unaffected by the network size, since these providers are
positioned near users. By dynamically learning real-time
forwarding information on backup providers, the approach
ensures efﬁcient data retrieval from optimal providers and
is hardly impacted by the network size, thereby enabling
scalability in large IoT environments.
VIII. CONCLUSION
This proposal presents a multipath and multisource content
transmission and caching solution for ICIoT. At the cur-
rent stage, we implement the proposal in the experimental
environment where the network size is ﬁxed and content is
delivered in plaintext, and aim to: 1) validate the feasibility and
effectiveness of the proposal; 2) observe whether multipaths
toward multisources can help improve content delivery success
rates; 3) observe whether stable paths can help reduce content
delivery delays and energy consumption; and 4) observe
whether forwarding information on backup providers can
help shorten content-user distances, alleviate content delivery
delays and energy consumption, and enhance success rates.
In real-world IoT applications, the network size may vary
and thousands of nodes may be involved. Moreover, plaintext
transmission poses security risks. Encryption can address
security concerns and handle malicious nodes, preventing data
tampering and leakage. That is, before transmission, data is
encrypted and sent as ciphertext over the network. Even if
malicious nodes intercept the ciphertext, they cannot decrypt
it. This ensures protection against data tampering and leakage.
In future work, we will implement the proposed framework
in large-scale real-world network environments with varying
scales, and employ encryption to safeguard data security.
